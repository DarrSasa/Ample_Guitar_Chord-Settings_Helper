[Tom Turpin](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Turpin?action=edit&redlink=1 "Tom Turpin \(halaman belum tersedia\)")'s 1904 composition _The Buffalo Rag_ , in a 1906 performance by[Vess Ossman](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vess_Ossman?action=edit&redlink=1 "Vess Ossman \(halaman belum tersedia\)"). Although this is a ragtime piece, Ossman played with classic banjo style. He fingerpicked gut strings using a technique similar to classical guitarists.
* [MusicBrainz instrument](https://musicbrainz.org/instrument/6bf88fc7-a235-4d95-a910-a4ce4a4853c6)
The earliest written indication of an instrument akin to the banjo is in the 17thcentury: Richard Jobson (1621) in describing[The Gambia](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Gambia "The Gambia"), wrote about an instrument which some consider to be similar to the banjo.
They have little varietie of instruments, that which is most common in use, is made of a great gourd, and a necke thereunto fastned, resembling, in some sort, our Bandora; but they have no manner of fret, and the strings they are either such as the place yeeldes or their invention can attaine to make, being very unapt to yeeld a sweete and musicall sound, notwithstanding with pinnes they winde and bring to agree in tunable notes, having not above sixe strings upon their greatest instrument.[[1]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjojstor-1)
The term _banjo_ has several etymological origins. One theory links it to the[Mandinka](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandinka_people?action=edit&redlink=1 "Mandinka people \(halaman belum tersedia\)")language which gives the name of[Banjul](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjul "Banjul"), capital of The Gambia. Another claim is a connection to the West African _[akonting](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akonting?action=edit&redlink=1 "Akonting \(halaman belum tersedia\)")_ : it is made with a long bamboo neck called a _bangoe_. The material for the neck, called _ban julo_ in the Mandinka language, again gives _banjul_. In this interpretation,_banjul_ became a sort of eponym for the akonting as it crossed[the Atlantic](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic "The Atlantic"). The instrument's name might also derive from the[Kimbundu](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimbundu?action=edit&redlink=1 "Kimbundu \(halaman belum tersedia\)")word _mbanza_ ,[[2]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-2)which is a loan word to the Portuguese language resulting in the term _banza_ ,[[1]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjojstor-1)which was used by early French travelers in the Americas.[[3]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-3)Its earliest recorded use was in 1678[[1]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjojstor-1)by the Sovereign Council of Martinique which reinstated a 1654 decree that placed prohibitions and restrictions on "dances and assemblies of negroes" deemed to be _[kalenda](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calinda?action=edit&redlink=1 "Calinda \(halaman belum tersedia\)")_ , which was defined as the gathering of enslaved Africans who danced to the sound of a drum and an instrument called the banza.[[1]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjojstor-1)[[4]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Dessalles_Vonglis-4)
The[OED](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_English_Dictionary "Oxford English Dictionary")claims that the term _banjo_ comes from a[dialectal](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect?action=edit&redlink=1 "Dialect \(halaman belum tersedia\)")pronunciation of Portuguese _bandore_ or from an early anglicisation of Spanish _[bandurria](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandurria?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bandurria \(halaman belum tersedia\)")_.[[5]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-5)Contrary evidence shows that the terms _bandore_ and _bandurria_ were used when Europeans encountered the instrument or its kin varieties in use by people of African descent, who used names for the instrument such as _banza_ ,[[1]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjojstor-1)as it was called in places such as[Haiti](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiti "Haiti"), varieties that were built around a[gourd](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourd?action=edit&redlink=1 "Gourd \(halaman belum tersedia\)")body with a wooden plank for the neck.[François Richard de Tussac](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Richard_de_Tussac?action=edit&redlink=1 "François Richard de Tussac \(halaman belum tersedia\)"), a former planter from[Saint-Domingue](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Domingue "Saint-Domingue"), details its construction in the book _Le Cri des Colons_ , published in 1810, stating:[[6]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RichardTussac-6)[[7]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-HaitianBanza-7)
As for the guitars, which the negroes call _banzas_ , this is what they consist of: they cut lengthwise, through the middle, a fresh[calabash](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabash?action=edit&redlink=1 "Calabash \(halaman belum tersedia\)")[the fruit of a tree called the[callebassier](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crescentia_cujete?action=edit&redlink=1 "Crescentia cujete \(halaman belum tersedia\)")]. This fruit is sometimes eight inches or more in diameter. The stretch across it the skin of a goat, which they attach on the edges with little nails; they put two or three little holes on this surface, and then a kind of plank or piece of wood that is rudely flattened makes the neck of the instrument; they stretch three strings made of pitre [a kind of string taken from the[agave](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agave "Agave")plant, commonly known as pitre] across it; and so the instrument is built. On this instrument they play[airs](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_\(music\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Air \(music\) \(halaman belum tersedia\)")composed of three or four notes, which they repeat constantly.[[6]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RichardTussac-6)[[7]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-HaitianBanza-7)
[Michel Étienne Descourtilz](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_%C3%89tienne_Descourtilz?action=edit&redlink=1 "Michel Étienne Descourtilz \(halaman belum tersedia\)"), a naturalist who visited Haiti in the early 1800s, described it as _banzas_ , a Negro instrument, that the natives prepare by sawing one of the calabashes or a large gourd lengthwise, to which they attach a neck and sonorous strings made from the filament" of aloe plants.[[8]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-8)It was played during any occasion, from boredom to joyous parties and[calendas](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calinda?action=edit&redlink=1 "Calinda \(halaman belum tersedia\)")to funeral ceremonies. It was the custom to also combine this sound with the more noisy _[bamboula](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboula?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bamboula \(halaman belum tersedia\)")_ , a type of drum made from a stick of bamboo covered on both sides with a skin that was played with fingers and knuckles while sitting astride.[[9]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-9)[[7]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-HaitianBanza-7)
Various instruments in Africa, chief among them the[ _kora_](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kora_\(instrument\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Kora \(instrument\) \(halaman belum tersedia\)"), feature a skin[drumhead](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drumhead?action=edit&redlink=1 "Drumhead \(halaman belum tersedia\)")and[gourd](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourd?action=edit&redlink=1 "Gourd \(halaman belum tersedia\)")(or similar shell) body.[[10]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Pestcoe-Adams-2010-10)These instruments differ from early African-American banjos in that the necks do not possess a Western-style fingerboard and tuning pegs; instead they have stick necks, with strings attached to the neck with loops for tuning.[[10]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Pestcoe-Adams-2010-10)
Another likely relative of the banjo is the aforementioned _akonting_ , a spike folk lute which is constructed using a gourd body, a long wooden neck, and three strings[[11]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-11)played by the[Jola tribe](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jola_people?action=edit&redlink=1 "Jola people \(halaman belum tersedia\)")of[Senegambia](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegambia "Senegambia"), and the _ubaw-akwala_ of the[Igbo](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibo_people?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ibo people \(halaman belum tersedia\)").[[12]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Chambers-2009-12)Similar instruments include the _[xalam](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xalam?action=edit&redlink=1 "Xalam \(halaman belum tersedia\)")_ of[Senegal](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegal "Senegal")[[13]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-FischerKelly2000-13)and the _[ngoni](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngoni_\(instrument\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ngoni \(instrument\) \(halaman belum tersedia\)")_ of the[Wassoulou](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassoulou?action=edit&redlink=1 "Wassoulou \(halaman belum tersedia\)")region that includes parts of[Mali](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali "Mali"),[Guinea](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea "Guinea"), and[Ivory Coast](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory_Coast "Ivory Coast"), as well as a larger variation of the _ngoni_ , known as the _gimbri_ , developed in[Morocco](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco "Morocco")by sub-Saharan Africans ([Gnawa](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gnawa "Gnawa")or[Haratin](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haratin?action=edit&redlink=1#Morocco "Haratin \(halaman belum tersedia\)")).
Banjo-like instruments seem to have been independently invented in several different places, in addition to the many African instruments mentioned above, since instruments similar to the banjo are known from a diverse array of distant countries. For example, the Chinese _[sanxian](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanxian "Sanxian")_ , the Japanese _[shamisen](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamisen "Shamisen")_ , the Persian _[tar](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tar_\(lute\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Tar \(lute\) \(halaman belum tersedia\)")_ , and the Moroccan _[sintir](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sintir?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sintir \(halaman belum tersedia\)")_.[[12]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Chambers-2009-12)
Banjos with fingerboards and tuning pegs are known from the Caribbean as early as the 17thcentury.[[10]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Pestcoe-Adams-2010-10)Some 18th- and early 19th-century writers transcribed the name of these instruments variously as _bangie_ ,_banza_ ,_bonjaw_ ,[[14]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-14)_banjer_[[15]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-15)and _banjar_.
The instrument became increasingly available commercially from around the second quarter of the 19th century due to[minstrel show](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minstrel_show?action=edit&redlink=1 "Minstrel show \(halaman belum tersedia\)")performances.[[16]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-grove-16)
In the[antebellum South](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antebellum_South?action=edit&redlink=1 "Antebellum South \(halaman belum tersedia\)"), many enslaved Africans played the banjo, spreading it to the rest of the population.[[1]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjojstor-1)In his memoir _With Sabre and Scalpel: The Autobiography of a Soldier and Surgeon_ , the Confederate veteran and surgeon[John Allan Wyeth](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Allan_Wyeth?action=edit&redlink=1 "John Allan Wyeth \(halaman belum tersedia\)")recalls learning to play the banjo as a child from an enslaved person on his family plantation.[[1]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjojstor-1)Another man who learned to play from African-Americans, probably in the 1820s, was[Joel Walker Sweeney](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Walker_Sweeney?action=edit&redlink=1 "Joel Walker Sweeney \(halaman belum tersedia\)"), a[minstrel](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minstrel_show?action=edit&redlink=1 "Minstrel show \(halaman belum tersedia\)")performer from[Appomattox Court House](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appomattox_Court_House_National_Historical_Park?action=edit&redlink=1 "Appomattox Court House National Historical Park \(halaman belum tersedia\)"),[Virginia](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia "Virginia").[[17]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Voloshin-17)[[18]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-2009Banjo-18)Sweeney has been credited with adding a string to the four-string African-American banjo, and popularizing the five-string banjo.[[17]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Voloshin-17)[[18]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-2009Banjo-18)Although[Robert McAlpin Williamson](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McAlpin_Williamson?action=edit&redlink=1 "Robert McAlpin Williamson \(halaman belum tersedia\)")is the first documented white banjoist,[[19]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-19)in the 1830s Sweeney became the first white performer to play the banjo on stage.[[17]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Voloshin-17)Sweeney's musical performances occurred at the beginning of the minstrel era, as banjos shifted away from being exclusively homemade folk instruments to instruments of a more modern style.[[20]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-AmerRhytm-20)Sweeney participated in this transition by encouraging drum maker William Boucher of[Baltimore](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore "Baltimore")to make banjos commercially for him to sell.[[18]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-2009Banjo-18)
In 1949, Arthur Woodward credited Sweeney with replacing the gourd with a wooden sound box covered in skin, and adding a short fifth string around 1831.[[21]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Bluestein-21)However, modern scholar[Gene Bluestein](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Bluestein?action=edit&redlink=1 "Gene Bluestein \(halaman belum tersedia\)")pointed out in 1964 that Sweeney may not have originated either the 5th string or sound box.[[21]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Bluestein-21)This new banjo was at first tuned d'Gdf♯a, though by the 1890s, this had been transposed up to g'cgbd'. Banjos were introduced in Britain by Sweeney's group, the American[Virginia Minstrels](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Minstrels?action=edit&redlink=1 "Virginia Minstrels \(halaman belum tersedia\)"), in the 1840s, and became very popular in[music halls](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_hall?action=edit&redlink=1 "Music hall \(halaman belum tersedia\)").[[22]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Zither-22)
The instrument grew in popularity during the 1840s after[Sweeney](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Sweeney?action=edit&redlink=1 "Joel Sweeney \(halaman belum tersedia\)")began his traveling minstrel show.[[23]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RTB6-23)By the end of the 1840s the instrument had expanded from Caribbean possession to take root in places across America and across the Atlantic in England.[[24]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-BOB1-24)[[25]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjoentc-25)It was estimated in 1866 that there were probably 10,000 banjos in[New York City](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_New_York "Kota New York"), up from only a handful in 1844. People were exposed to banjos not only at minstrel shows, but also medicine shows, Wild-West shows, variety shows, and traveling vaudeville shows.[[26]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RTB1-26)The banjo's popularity also was given a boost by the Civil War, as servicemen on both sides in the Army or Navy were exposed to the banjo played in minstrel shows and by other servicemen.[[27]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RTB2-27)A popular movement of aspiring banjoists began as early as 1861.[[28]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Kansas-28)The enthusiasm for the instrument was labeled a "banjo craze" or "banjo mania."[[28]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Kansas-28)
[Carnival of Venice](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_of_Venice_\(song\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Carnival of Venice \(song\) \(halaman belum tersedia\)"), variations on the folk song composed by Julius Benedict, arranged for banjo and played on banjo by[Alfred A. Farland](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_A._Farland?action=edit&redlink=1 "Alfred A. Farland \(halaman belum tersedia\)"). This song is an example of Farland's use of bare fingers to produce[tremolo](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremolo?action=edit&redlink=1 "Tremolo \(halaman belum tersedia\)")to get long notes from the instrument (much as the cornet or violin can naturally play.)
The _Briggs Banjo Method_ , considered to be the first banjo method and which taught the _stroke style_ of playing, also mentioned the existence of another way of playing, the _guitar style._[[34]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Stewart1-34)[[35]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjoent3-35)Alternatively known as "finger style", the new way of playing the banjo displaced the stroke method, until by 1870 it was the dominant style.[[36]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RTB3-36)Although mentioned by Briggs, it wasn't taught. The first banjo method to teach the technique was _Frank B. Converse's New and Complete Method for the Banjo with or without a Master_ , published in 1865.[[37]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RTB4-37)[[38]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjoent4-38)
In the guitar style of Banjo-playing...the little finger of the right hand is rested upon the head near the bridge...[and] serves as a rest to the hand and a resistance to the movement of picking the strings...In the beginning it is best to acquire a knowledge of picking the strings with the use of the first and second fingers and thumb only, allowing the third finger to remain idle until the other fingers have become thoroughly accustomed to their work...the three fingers are almost invariably used in playing chords and accompaniments to songs."[[34]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Stewart1-34)
Banjo, from the Musical Instruments series (N82) for Duke brand cigarettes, 1888
The banjo, although popular, carried low-class associations from its role in[blackface](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackface?action=edit&redlink=1 "Blackface \(halaman belum tersedia\)")minstrel shows, medicine shows, tent shows, and variety shows or vaudeville.[[39]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-olive1-39)There was a push in the 19th century to bring the instrument into "respectability."[[39]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-olive1-39)Musicians such as[William A. Huntley](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_A._Huntley?action=edit&redlink=1 "William A. Huntley \(halaman belum tersedia\)")made an effort to "elevate" the instrument or make it more "artistic," by "bringing it to a more sophisticated level of technique and repertoire based on European standards."[[40]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjoent11-40)Huntley may have been the first white performer to successfully make the transition from performing in blackface to being himself on stage, noted by the[Boston Herald](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Herald "Boston Herald")in November 1884.[[40]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjoent11-40)He was supported by another former blackface performer, Samuel Swaim Stewart, in his corporate magazine that popularized highly talented professionals.[[41]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjoent8-41)
As the "raucous" imitations of plantation life decreased in minstrelsy, the banjo became more acceptable as an instrument of fashionable society, even to be accepted into women's parlors.[[23]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RTB6-23)[[42]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjoent13-42)Part of that change was a switch from the stroke style to the guitar playing style.[[23]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RTB6-23)[[42]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjoent13-42)[[37]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-RTB4-37)An 1888 newspaper said, "All the maidens and a good many of the women also strum the instrument, banjo classes abound on every side and banjo recitals are among the newest diversions of fashion...Youths and elderly men too have caught the fever...the star strummers among men are in demand at the smartest parties and have the choosing of the society of the most charming girls."[[43]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-banjoent9-43)
New styles of playing, a new look, instruments in a variety of pitch ranges to take the place of different sections in an orchestra – all helped to separate the instrument from the rough minstrel image of the previous 50–60 years.[[45]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-museumsign-45)The instrument was modern now, a bright new thing, with polished metal sides.[[45]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-museumsign-45)
The instruments became ornately decorated in the[1920s](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roaring_Twenties?action=edit&redlink=1 "Roaring Twenties \(halaman belum tersedia\)")to be visually dynamic to a theater audience.[[46]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-mushistpage-46)The instruments were increasingly modified or made in a new style – necks that were shortened to handle the four steel (not fiber as before) strings, strings that were sounded with a pick instead of fingers, four strings instead of five and tuned differently.[[46]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-mushistpage-46)The changes reflected the nature of post-World-War-I music.[[46]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-mushistpage-46)The country was turning away from European classics, preferring the "upbeat and carefree feel" of[jazz](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musik_jaz "Musik jaz"), and American soldiers returning from the war helped to drive this change.[[46]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-mushistpage-46)
The change in tastes toward dance music and the need for louder instruments began a few years before the war, however, with ragtime.[[46]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-mushistpage-46)That music encouraged musicians to alter their 5-string banjos to four, add the louder steel strings and use a pick or plectrum, all in an effort to be heard over the brass and reed instruments that were current in dance-halls.[[46]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-mushistpage-46)The four string plectrum and tenor banjos did not eliminate the five-string variety. They were products of their times and musical purposes—ragtime and jazz dance music and theater music.
Earl Scruggs was seen both as a legend and a "contemporary musical innovator" who gave his name to his style of playing, the _Scruggs Style_.[[49]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Scruggssign-49)Scruggs played the banjo "with heretofore unheard of speed and dexterity," using a picking technique for the 5-string banjo that he perfected from 2-finger and 3-finger picking techniques in rural North Carolina.[[49]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Scruggssign-49)His playing reached Americans through the[Grand Ole Opry](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ole_Opry "Grand Ole Opry")and into the living rooms of Americans who didn't listen to country or bluegrass music, through the theme music of _[The Beverly Hillbillies](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beverly_Hillbillies?action=edit&redlink=1 "The Beverly Hillbillies \(halaman belum tersedia\)")_ TV[sitcom](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitcom "Sitcom").[[49]](https://id.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjo#cite_note-Scruggssign-49)
