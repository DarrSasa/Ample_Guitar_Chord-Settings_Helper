There are hypotheses that point to the influence on flamenco of types of dance from the Indian subcontinent; the place of origin of the[Romani people](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people?action=edit&redlink=1 "Romani people \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)").[[1]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-Leblon-1)The North Indian scales of flamenco were introduced to Andalusia by the Romani migrations from[Northern India](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_India?action=edit&redlink=1 "North India \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)").[[13]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-13)These Roma migrants also brought bells, and an extensive repertoire of songs and dances.[[14]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-14)Upon arrival to Andalusia in the 15th century, they were exposed to the rich Arab-Andalusian music culture, itself a hybrid of Spanish music tradition going back to the 8th century with the establishment of[Al-Andalus](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus?action=edit&redlink=1 "Al-Andalus \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"), which brought in traditions and music from the[Arabian Peninsula](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Peninsula?action=edit&redlink=1 "Arabian Peninsula \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"),[Northern Africa](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Africa?action=edit&redlink=1 "North Africa \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")and[Sephardic](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardic_Jews?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sephardic Jews \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")features. Some of the instruments associated with flamenco and Spanish folklore in different regions today, are the wooden[castanets](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castanets?action=edit&redlink=1 "Castanets \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")and[tambourines](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tambourines?action=edit&redlink=1 "Tambourines \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"), both believed to originate during the Al Andalus period.[[15]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-15)[[16]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-16)This centuries-long period of cultural intermingling, formed the roots of flamenco song and dance genres.[[17]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-17)[[18]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-18)
In 1881[Silverio Franconetti](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverio_Franconetti?action=edit&redlink=1 "Silverio Franconetti \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")opened the first flamenco singer café in Seville. In Silverio's café the cantaores were in a very competitive environment, which allowed the emergence of the professional cantaor and served as a crucible where flamenco art was configured. Locals learned the cantes, while reinterpreting the Andalusian folk songs in their own style, expanding the repertoire. Likewise, the taste of the public contributed to configure the flamenco genre, unifying its technique and its theme.[[23]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-23)
The musical representative José Antonio Pulpón was a decisive character in that fusion, as he urged the cantaor Agujetas to collaborate with the Sevillian Andalusian rock group "[Pata Negra](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pata_Negra?action=edit&redlink=1 "Pata Negra \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")", the most revolutionary couple since Antonio Chacón and[Ramón Montoya](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Montoya?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ramón Montoya \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"), initiating a new path for flamenco. It also fostered the artistic union between the virtuoso guitarist from Algeciras[Paco de Lucía](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paco_de_Luc%C3%ADa?action=edit&redlink=1 "Paco de Lucía \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")and the long-standing singer from the island[Camarón de la Isla](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camar%C3%B3n_de_la_Isla?action=edit&redlink=1 "Camarón de la Isla \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"), who gave a creative impulse to flamenco that would mean its definitive break with Mairena's conservatism. When both artists undertook their solo careers, Camarón became a mythical cantaor for his art and personality, with a legion of followers, while Paco de Lucía reconfigured the entire musical world of flamenco, opening up to new influences, such as Brazilian music, Arabic and jazz and introducing new musical instruments such as the Peruvian cajon, the transverse flute, etc.
The compás is fundamental to flamenco. Compás is most often translated as[rhythm](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm?action=edit&redlink=1 "Rhythm \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")but it demands far more precise interpretation than any other Western style of music. If there is no guitarist available, the compás is rendered through hand clapping (_palmas_) or by hitting a table with the knuckles. The guitarist uses techniques like strumming (_rasgueado_) or tapping the[soundboard](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_board_\(music\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sound board \(music\) \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")(_golpe_). Changes of chords emphasize the most important downbeats.[[35]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-35)
The[Bulerías](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buler%C3%ADas?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bulerías \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")is the emblematic palo of flamenco: today its 12-beat cycle is most often played with accents on the 3rd, 6th, 8th, 10th and 12th beats. The accompanying _palmas_ are played in groups of 6 beats, giving rise to a multitude of counter-rhythms and percussive voices within the 12 beat compás. In certain regions like, Jerez, Spain, the rhythm stays in a simpler six-count rhythm, only including the twelve count in a musical resolve. This is like starting the counting at 9 so it goes this way: 9**10** 11**12** 1 2 - again, bold means the emphasis when clapping. In real life it is counted from 1 like: 1**2** 3**4** 5 6 and so on.
Although flamenco is often described as a folk, popular or oral tradition, many scholars and performers also treat it as a complex musical system. Its sophistication lies not only in vocal intensity or dance virtuosity, but also in the interaction of[mode](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_mode?action=edit&redlink=1 "Musical mode \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"), harmony,[compás](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comp%C3%A1s_\(flamenco\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Compás \(flamenco\) \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"), melodic variation, improvisation, guitar technique and formal conventions. The musical identity of a _palo_ may depend simultaneously on its rhythmic cycle, melodic contour, cadential formulas, stanzaic form, local tradition and performance practice.[[36]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-36)
The[flamenco guitar](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flamenco_guitar?action=edit&redlink=1 "Flamenco guitar \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")adds another layer of technical and musical elaboration. Flamenco guitarists must combine accompaniment, percussion, harmonic support, melodic interludes and rhythmic control. Techniques such as _rasgueado_ ,_alzapúa_ ,_picado_ , tremolo, golpes and thumb work are not merely decorative; they articulate the compás, answer the singer, cue the dancer and create solo passages called _falsetas_. Modern flamenco guitar has also expanded the harmonic vocabulary of the tradition through chord substitution, modulation, new guitar positions and concert-style solo composition.([Manuel 2006](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#CITEREFManuel2006), 96–98)harv error: no target: CITEREFManuel2006 ([help](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D8%B2%D9%85%D8%B1%D9%88:Harv_and_Sfn_template_errors?action=edit&redlink=1 "زمرو:Harv and Sfn template errors \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"))
Flamenco's appeal to classical audiences has also been reinforced by the development of concert flamenco guitar. Guitarists such as[Ramón Montoya](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Montoya?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ramón Montoya \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"),[Sabicas](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabicas?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sabicas \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)"),[Paco Peña](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paco_Pe%C3%B1a?action=edit&redlink=1 "Paco Peña \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")and[Paco de Lucía](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paco_de_Luc%C3%ADa?action=edit&redlink=1 "Paco de Lucía \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")helped present flamenco guitar as a solo concert instrument as well as an accompaniment for song and dance. Carnegie Hall describes Paco de Lucía as one of the world's most celebrated flamenco guitarists and notes that his work embraced traditional, classical, jazz and contemporary elements.[[41]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-41)His 1991 recording of Joaquín Rodrigo's _[Concierto de Aranjuez](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concierto_de_Aranjuez?action=edit&redlink=1 "Concierto de Aranjuez \(صفحو وجود نٿو رکي\)")_ became a notable example of a flamenco guitarist entering a canonical classical-guitar repertory.[[42]](https://sd.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D9%81%D9%84%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%8A%D9%86%DA%AA%D9%88#cite_note-42)
