**Pizzicato** means: playing a[string instrument](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_instrument "String instrument")by plucking the strings (pulling a string with the[finger](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger "Finger")and letting go quickly). Instruments such as the[violin](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin "Violin"),[viola](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viola "Viola"),[cello](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cello "Cello")and[double bass](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bass "Double bass")are normally played with a[bow](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_\(music\) "Bow \(music\)"), but if the[composer](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composer "Composer")wants the player to pluck instead of bow, the word “pizzicato” or just “pizz” is written in the music. When the player has to play with the bow again the word “arco” is written in the music (“arco” is the Italian word for “bow”).
When instruments of the violin family are played pizzicato the player usually plucks the string with the[index finger](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger "Finger")(pointing finger) of the right hand somewhere over the fingerboard. Bassists typically use the index and middle finger. Sometimes the player may rest the thumb on the edge of the fingerboard to keep the hand steady. Sometimes more fingers can be used for particularly fast pizzicato sections. Cellists and double bass players can use the[thumb](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thumb "Thumb")for plucking, especially for playing[chords](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chord "Chord").
It is also possible to play pizzicato with the left hand (the hand which is normally doing the fingering). It is not difficult to pluck an open string with the left hand. Stopped strings are harder, and the brilliant violinist and composer[Niccolò Paganini](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niccol%C3%B2_Paganini "Niccolò Paganini")wrote some[virtuoso](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtuoso "Virtuoso")pieces with extremely difficult left hand pizzicato.
Very occasionally violinists may be asked to pluck their instruments holding them down in their laps. This is normally when they are deliberately imitating a[guitar](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar "Guitar").
One special effect can be made by pulling the string hard and letting it go so that it snaps against the fingerboard.[Bartók](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9la_Bart%C3%B3k "Béla Bartók")used this effect several times. This is not the same as jazz bass players who slap the strings at the end of a note (“slap bass”).
In orchestras composers used pizzicato in the[17th century](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_century "17th century").[Monteverdi](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Monteverdi "Claudio Monteverdi")used it in his opera _Il combattimento di Tancredi e Clorinda_. In the[19th century](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_century "19th century")the[Romantic](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_music "Romantic music")composers often asked for pizzicato.[Tchaikovsky](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchaikovsky "Tchaikovsky")’s _Symphony no 4_ has a whole[scherzo](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scherzo "Scherzo")[movement](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_\(music\) "Movement \(music\)")for pizzicato strings.[Johann Strauss](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Strauss_II "Johann Strauss II")wrote a _Pizzicato Polka_ and in the[20th century](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_century "20th century")[Britten](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Britten "Benjamin Britten")wrote a whole movement for pizzicato strings in his _Simple Symphony_.
