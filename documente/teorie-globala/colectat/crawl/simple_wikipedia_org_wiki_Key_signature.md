There are twelve major and twelve minor keys (properly called “modes"). This is because a scale can start on any note, and there are twelve notes within an[octave](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octave "Octave")(on a piano keyboard, seven white keyboard keys and five black keyboard keys). Each major key has its own key signature. The relationship between the keys is explained in the article "[circle of fifths](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_of_fifths "Circle of fifths")". Each minor key shares a key signature with one of the major keys.
One can tell from the key signature what key a piece is in so long as one knows whether it is major or minor (see “[mode](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mode_\(music\) "Mode \(music\)")”). For example: a key signature of one sharp is either G major or E minor. The best way to find out which it is would be to see whether the end of the piece is based on G major or E minor. Also: if the piece is in the minor key it is very likely that there will be several accidentals in the piece which sharpen the 7th note of the scale (in this case changing a lot of the Ds to D sharps).
